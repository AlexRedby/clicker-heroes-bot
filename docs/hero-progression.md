# Hero progression

At initial start and after a confirmed Ascension, `-progression` sweeps from the top for missing hires and level-unavailable upgrades. OpenCV recognizes button captions and searches each upgrade strip for the dark unavailable placeholder. An owned hero is leveled only when that placeholder is present. Purchased checks, total upgrade count, hero names and numerical levels are not read. Gray artwork and unlocked unpurchased icons are skipped. Affordable visible hires take priority over owned rows. HIRE uses one x1 click; owned rows with a level-unavailable upgrade use Q/MAX to buy the maximum affordable levels. Both receive at most two inputs per viewport before the cursor advances; the attempt limit prevents a missed input from blocking the sweep. The bulk footer is handled after the sweep, without an initial upgrade purchase pass.

The sweep scrolls with overlap. Submitting a scroll resets the viewport cursor before the next analysis, so the new screen is not filtered with old Y coordinates. Observed movement is still required before using its purchase or completion decision. F8 preserves the cursor and attempt count; Ascension resets them. A disabled HIRE button and a recognized passive hero complete the affordable sweep without gold or price OCR. Reaching the bottom also completes the affordable sweep. Unfinished locked rows and failed hires are revisited after a minute of ordinary play, without another export or a separate progression mode. Auto-progression is enabled during preparation, without waiting for passive DPS. Whenever purchase and navigation work is unavailable, the shared action queue assists monster clicks; unreadable hero numbers do not block earning gold. Startup shares the capture/action pipeline and covering modals block its input.

Ordinary play buys levels for the latest available hero, prioritizes an affordable successor and saves for the next one when its price is within ten times current gold. It does not revisit earlier heroes or estimate DPS efficiency.


The bulk upgrade footer is checked every 30 seconds on shared frames, with a plain click when upgrades are available. A recognized disabled footer needs no click. As part of progression, one available owned clicker is reserved for the footer when more than one is owned; the others go to the monster. A recognized visible footer, including a disabled one, can receive its clicker immediately during hero preparation. Once the footer assignment is confirmed by a decreased free count, ordinary bulk OCR checks are unnecessary. Existing assignments are preserved, no clickers are purchased, and failed/unconfirmed footer placement retains ordinary clicks.

## Ordinary latest-hero play

1. Require the full-screen Heroes tab and recognizable quantity bar on the primary display. A changed or unknown layout skips the attempt. A complete recognized upgrade footer proves the end of a short list without a scrollbar.
2. Drag the scrollbar thumb only when it is above the bottom, and verify its final position. Scrolling does not depend on the selected purchase quantity.
3. Keep `x1` selected; if another quantity is selected, cycle with `T` until the orange `x1` state is visible. This keyboard-only action does not wait for fish analysis. Each button then shows a numeric price instead of `MAX`. A blue button identifies the deepest available candidate. A following dark row with a level means a later owned hero cannot be leveled, so the bot waits. A following unowned row must have a clear empty level area. A missing or obscured successor of an owned candidate skips the attempt.
4. Run Tesseract on cropped gold, price and level text regions. Gold and levels use white text masks. Prices use a yellow text mask with brightness adjusted for disabled buttons, a crop that excludes the coin and button border, and smooth enlargement. Level recognition isolates the text line and requires a readable `Lvl` label and integer value. Hero names are compared as static image regions to keep the selected row consistent across captures.
5. Enable **Always use scientific notation** in the game. Accept ordinary values with up to three decimal places and normalized scientific mantissas between one and ten. Compare base-10 logarithms so large exponents do not overflow a floating-point amount. Zero gold is represented as negative infinity.
6. For an owned hero, wait if the next price is at most ten times gold; otherwise hold `Q`, click the identified hero button, and release `Q`. A newly available unowned hero is hired first with an x1 click. Q supplies the temporary MAX modifier without a selection screenshot; the persistent quantity stays at `x1`. The key is released even if input fails or the run is cancelled.
7. Read the level before and after a purchase, moving the pointer away from hero buttons before the confirmation capture to dismiss tooltips. Only a larger readable integer on the same row confirms success. Level-up requires a stable viewport; hiring may expand the list or create a scrollbar. Overlay or animation pixel differences alone cannot confirm a purchase. After confirmation, read a fresh purchase decision immediately; there is no fixed five-second pause between successful purchases.

OCR reads inherit run cancellation and have a three-second execution timeout, configurable with `-ocr-timeout`; waiting for an OCR slot does not consume that execution budget. At most two Tesseract processes run, each with `OMP_THREAD_LIMIT=1`. Only an unreadable text result retries the gold mask, not a process timeout. The shared capture pipeline runs fish, HUD and due hero analysis independently. Gold, price and baseline level use the same frame with at most two concurrent OCR processes. Post-purchase confirmation reads only level and row stability from subsequent shared frames. It does not trigger private fish scans; a final failed confirmation waits for a shared fish observation from that frame or later so obstruction is not counted as purchase failure. Pause/resume invalidates pending input decisions. Q is held only during the purchase action and released before confirmation or any fish click.

Unreadable text or ambiguous rows skip purchases. Live checks must still cover locked next-hero prices in `x1`, window scaling, scrolling, pause/resume, and the transition to a newly available hero.
